## CPL Article 1 — Short Title & Definitions

- **CPL 1.20 (criminal action)** — A criminal action commences when an accusatory instrument is filed against a defendant in a criminal court. It runs until sentence is imposed or another final disposition.
- **CPL 1.20 (criminal proceeding)** — Broader than "criminal action": any proceeding that's a stage of a criminal action, OR any proceeding in a criminal court relating to a prospective, pending, or already-completed criminal action.
- **CPL 1.20 (accusatory instrument)** — The umbrella term: an indictment, a superior court information (SCI), a prosecutor's information, an information, a simplified information, a misdemeanor complaint, or a felony complaint.
- **CPL 1.20 (local crim. ct. accusatory instrument)** — Everything on the accusatory-instrument list except an indictment or a superior court information (SCI).
- **CPL 1.20 (superior ct. accusatory instrument)** — Only an indictment or a superior court information (SCI).
- **CPL 1.20 (petty offense)** — A violation or a traffic infraction. Not a "crime."
- **CPL 1.20 (crime)** — A misdemeanor or a felony only.
- **CPL 1.20 (judgment)** — A conviction plus the sentence imposed on it.

## CPL Article 10 — Courts: Local vs. Superior

- **CPL 10.10(2)** *[Sample Q]* — "Superior Court" means the supreme court or a county court. Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).
- **CPL 10.10 (local criminal court)** — District Court, NYC Criminal Court, a City Court, a Town Court, or a Village Court — plus a superior court judge sitting as a local criminal court.
- **CPL 10.20 (superior court jurisdiction)** — Trial jurisdiction over indictments — only a superior court can try a felony to verdict — plus original jurisdiction over all offenses.
- **CPL 10.30 (local criminal court jurisdiction)** — Trial jurisdiction only over petty offenses and misdemeanors. Still handles felonies at the front end — arraignment, bail, felony hearing ("preliminary jurisdiction") — but can't try one to verdict.

## CPL Article 30 — Speedy Trial & Limitations

- **CPL 30.10(2)** — Periods of limitation to commence a prosecution: Class A felony — none, ever. Any other felony — 5 years. Misdemeanor — 2 years. Petty offense (violation) — 1 year.
- **CPL 30.30(1)** — Prosecution must be ready for trial within: felony — 6 months. Class A misdemeanor — 90 days. Class B misdemeanor — 60 days. Violation only — 30 days.

## CPL Article 100 — Accusatory Instruments (Local Court)

- **CPL 100.10** — Four local-court accusatory instruments: information (accuses of a misdemeanor or lesser, the highest bar), simplified information (abbreviated, mainly traffic infractions and similar), misdemeanor complaint (accuses of a misdemeanor on a lower evidentiary bar, gets you to arraignment only), felony complaint (commences a felony case in local court, preliminary stages only).
- **CPL 100.40** — An information is sufficient on its face only with non-hearsay allegations establishing a prima facie case for every element of the offense. A misdemeanor or felony complaint only needs reasonable cause — a lower standard.
- **CPL 100.40 (felony complaint role)** — A misdemeanor complaint has to be replaced by an information (or the defendant waives that) before the case can move past arraignment. A felony complaint serves only a preliminary function until superseded by an indictment.

## CPL Article 110 — Requiring Appearance for Arraignment

- **CPL 110.10(1)** — Where a criminal action has already been commenced (an accusatory instrument filed) and the defendant hasn't been arraigned or come under the court's control, appearance can be compelled by: a warrant of arrest (Art. 120), a summons (Art. 130), or extradition-type procedures for a defendant not at liberty in the state (Arts. 560, 570, 580, 590, 600).
- **CPL 110.10(2)** — Where NO criminal action has yet been commenced, appearance can be compelled by: an arrest without a warrant (Art. 140), or an appearance ticket (Art. 150) — with the accusatory instrument then filed at or before that appearance.

## CPL Article 120 — Warrant of Arrest

- **CPL 120.20(1)** — After an accusatory instrument is filed (other than a simplified traffic information) against a defendant not yet arraigned: if the instrument is sufficient on its face, the court may issue a warrant of arrest. If it's not sufficient on its face and can't be fixed, the court must dismiss it instead.
- **CPL 120.30(1)** — A warrant of arrest may be issued only by the local criminal court (or youth part) where the underlying accusatory instrument was actually filed, and it's returnable only in that same court.
- **CPL 120.70** — Where executable: a warrant from a district court, NYC Criminal Court, a superior court's youth part, or a superior court judge sitting as a local court is executable anywhere in the state. A warrant from a city, town, or village court is executable only in the county of issuance or an adjoining county, unless endorsed in writing by another local court.

## CPL Article 160 — Sealing Records After Favorable Termination

- **CPL 160.50(1)** *[Sample Q]* — When a criminal action terminates in the defendant's favor, the record is sealed automatically — unless the district attorney (or the court on its own motion) moves to block sealing with at least 5 days' notice to the defendant or their attorney, and the court finds the interests of justice require otherwise. Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026).

## CPL Article 170 — ACD — Adjournment in Contemplation of Dismissal

- **CPL 170.55** — General ACD (adjournment in contemplation of dismissal) — available for eligible misdemeanor and lesser charges. On motion (by either side with the other's consent, or by the court with both sides' consent), the court adjourns the case without date, aimed at eventual dismissal. Maximum adjournment: 6 months, or 1 year if it's a family offense.
- **CPL 170.56** *[Sample Q]* — Marihuana-specific ACD — a narrower version limited to certain low-level marihuana violations (Penal Law 222.10 etc.). Maximum adjournment: 12 months. If the case isn't restored to the calendar in that time, the accusatory instrument is deemed dismissed and the arrest/prosecution is treated as a nullity. Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026).

## CPL Article 180 — Felony Complaint: Arraignment Through Disposition

- **CPL 180.10** — At arraignment on a felony complaint, the defendant must be told the primary purpose of the proceeding is to determine whether they'll be held for grand jury action. The defendant has a right to a prompt hearing on that question.
- **CPL 180.70** — At the felony hearing, the standard is reasonable cause (same standard as a misdemeanor complaint, not an information's higher prima facie bar). Reasonable cause found → defendant held for the grand jury. No reasonable cause for any offense → complaint dismissed, defendant discharged or bail exonerated. Reasonable cause only for a lesser non-felony offense → court may reduce the charge with the DA's consent and if in the interest of justice, but never for a Class A felony or an armed felony.

## CPL Article 190 — Grand Jury Proceedings

- **CPL 190.25(1)** — A grand jury proceeding isn't valid unless at least 16 members are present. Finding an indictment — or any other official grand jury action, including a dismissal of charges — requires the concurrence of at least 12 members.

## CPL Article 195 — Waiver of Indictment

- **CPL 195.10(1)** — A defendant may waive indictment and be prosecuted by a superior court information (SCI) instead when: (a) a local court has held them for grand jury action, (b) they're not charged with a Class A felony punishable by death or life imprisonment, and (c) the DA consents. The waiver must happen before the grand jury actually files an indictment — once indicted, it's too late.

## CPL Article 200 — Indictment (Form & Joinder)

- **CPL 200.20(5)** *[Sample Q]* — Two or more indictments against the same defendant must be consolidated when they're joinable on the "same act or transaction" ground. Consolidation is only discretionary (not mandatory) for indictments joinable on other grounds, like offenses "similar in law." Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).

## CPL Article 210 — Indictment to Plea (Dismissal Motions)

- **CPL 210.20(1)** — A court may dismiss an indictment (or a count of one) on several enumerated grounds, including: the indictment is defective, the grand jury evidence was legally insufficient, the grand jury proceeding itself was defective, the defendant has immunity, or the prosecution is barred by a prior prosecution (double jeopardy). Parallels the dismissal grounds under CPLR 3211.
- **CPL 210.30** — A motion to dismiss (or reduce) an indictment for legally insufficient grand jury evidence must be preceded or accompanied by a motion to inspect the grand jury minutes. The court must grant inspection unless good cause exists to deny it.

## CPL Article 220 — The Plea (Guilty Pleas)

- **CPL 220.10** — Pleading not guilty, or guilty to the ENTIRE indictment, is a matter of right — no permission needed. Pleading guilty to only PART of the indictment, or to a lesser included offense, requires both the court's permission AND the DA's consent.

## CPL Article 260 — Order of a Jury Trial

- **CPL 260.30** — Order of a jury trial: jury selected and sworn → court's preliminary instructions → People's opening address → defendant's opening (optional) → People's case → defendant's case (optional) → rebuttal → summations: the DEFENDANT goes first, then the People → court's charge to the jury → deliberation and verdict. Trap to remember: openings go People-first, but summations go defendant-first, People-last.
- **CPL 260.10** — Every trial of an indictment must be a jury trial, except where the defendant has validly waived a jury under CPL 320.10.

## CPL Article 270 — Jury Selection: Challenges

- **CPL 270.25(2)** — Peremptory challenges per side: 20 if the top charge is a Class A felony, 15 if Class B or C felony, 10 for everything else — plus 2 more for each alternate juror. (Compare: civil cases under CPLR 4109 get only 3 combined, plus 1 per 2 alternates.)

## CPL Article 280 — Motion for a Mistrial

- **CPL 280.10** — Three grounds where the court MUST declare a mistrial: (1) defendant's motion — any error, defect, or conduct (in or out of the courtroom) that's prejudicial and deprives the defendant of a fair trial; (2) People's motion — gross misconduct specifically by the defendant (or someone acting for him) or by a juror, causing substantial and irreparable prejudice to the People's case (narrower than the defendant's ground — ordinary error doesn't qualify here); (3) either party's motion, or the court's own motion — physically impossible to proceed with the trial according to law.

## CPL Article 310 — Jury Notes (CPL 310.30 / O'Rama)

- **CPL 310.30** — When a deliberating jury sends a note asking for further instruction or information, the court must return the jury and, after notice to both sides, give the requested response. Per People v. O'Rama, the court's two "core responsibilities" are: give meaningful notice of the note's actual specific content to counsel before responding, and give a meaningful response to what the jury actually asked. Skipping meaningful notice is a "mode of proceedings" error — reversible even without a defense objection at trial.

## CPL Article 320 — Waiver of Jury Trial

- **CPL 320.10** *[Sample Q]* — Except for a charge of murder in the first degree, a defendant may waive a jury trial and be tried by the judge alone. The waiver must be in writing, signed by the defendant in person, in open court, in the presence of the court, with the court's approval — no prosecutor consent is required in New York. The court must approve unless the waiver is a stratagem for advantage or the defendant doesn't understand the consequences; if disapproved, the court must state its reasons on the record. Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).

## CPL Article 330 — Motion to Set Aside a Verdict

- **CPL 330.30** — After a guilty verdict but before sentencing, the defendant (only) may move to set aside the verdict on three grounds: (1) any ground that would require reversal on appeal as a matter of law; (2) improper conduct by (or toward) a juror, occurring outside the court's presence, that may have affected a substantial right and wasn't known to the defendant before the verdict; (3) newly discovered evidence that couldn't have been produced at trial with due diligence and probably would have led to a more favorable verdict.

## CPL Article 340 — Modes of Trial (Local Court Informations)

- **CPL 340.40** — Modes of trial for an information in local criminal court: the default is a single-judge (bench) trial. But a defendant who pleads not guilty to a misdemeanor information must be given a jury trial (conducted under Article 360), unless they waive it the same way as under CPL 320.10.

## CPL Article 360 — Jury Trial (Local Court) & Jury Size

- **CPL 360.10(1)** — A misdemeanor trial jury in a local criminal court consists of 6 jurors (alternates may also be selected). By contrast, a felony trial jury on an indictment in superior court is 12 jurors — a matter of constitutional/common-law tradition rather than one specific CPL section.

## CPL Article 380 — Sentencing: Statements & Timing

- **CPL 380.30(1)** — Sentence must be pronounced without unreasonable delay.
- **CPL 380.50** — Order of statements at sentencing: the prosecutor speaks first, then defense counsel, then the defendant personally (the court must ask the defendant whether they wish to speak). For a FELONY sentencing specifically, if the victim requests at least 10 days before the sentencing date, the court must also let the victim make a statement — and it must come before defense counsel's or the defendant's statements.
  - **Verified 2026-09-28** against the official text (nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CPL/380.50, revision dated 2026-06-05). The guide is correct. Official text adds three details the guide leaves out:
    - **2(b):** the court must notify the defendant **no less than 7 days** before sentencing that the victim intends to speak.
    - **2(c):** "Any statement by the victim must precede any statement by counsel to the defendant or the defendant," and **the defendant has the right to rebut** the victim's statement.
    - **2(a):** "victim" means the victim named in the accusatory instrument, or, if that person is unable or unwilling to speak, disabled, or deceased, **a family member or legal guardian** (unless the court finds it inappropriate).

## CPL Article 390 — Presentence Investigation Report (PSI)

- **CPL 390.20(1)-(2)** — Presentence investigation report (PSI): mandatory for any felony conviction — the court cannot pronounce sentence until it receives the written report. For a misdemeanor conviction, a PSI isn't required generally, but the court can't impose probation, imprisonment over 180 days, or consecutive sentences aggregating more than 90 days without ordering and receiving one first. The court may also order a PSI in any case, at its discretion.
