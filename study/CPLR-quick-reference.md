## CPLR Article 2 — Limitations of Time

- **CPLR 214** — Ordinary personal injury (negligence) — 3 years.
- **CPLR 213** — Contract / fraud — 6 years.
- **CPLR 214-a** — Medical malpractice — 2 years 6 months.
- **CPLR 215** — Intentional torts (assault, battery, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution, libel, slander) — 1 year.
- **CPLR 205** *[Corrected]* — Case dismissed and needs refiling → plaintiff gets 6 months to refile, except when the case ended by: (1) voluntary discontinuance, (2) failure to obtain personal jurisdiction over the defendant, (3) dismissal for neglect to prosecute, or (4) final judgment on the merits. Those four get no 6-month grace period.
- **CPLR 210(a)** — Claimant dies before the deadline expires → estate gets 1 year from the date of death to sue.
- **CPLR 210(b)** — Defendant dies → clock pauses for 18 months.
- **CPLR 208 (non-malpractice)** — Infancy toll — clock frozen until 18, then the normal period runs fresh (e.g. a 3-year claim → sue by 21). No 10-year cap applies here.
- **CPLR 208 (malpractice)** — Infancy + medical/dental/podiatric malpractice — no "wait until 18"; flat 10-year cap from the act itself, regardless of the child's age.
- **CPLR Insanity toll** — Automatic like infancy? No. Must prove an "overall inability to function in society," not just a diagnosis.

## CPLR Article 3 — Jurisdiction & Service

- **CPLR 308** — 5 methods to serve an individual, in hierarchy: (1) personal delivery — complete immediately; (2) suitable age/discretion + mail — complete 10 days after filing proof; (3) service on a designated agent; (4) "nail and mail" (only if 1 & 2 fail with due diligence) — affix + mail within 20 days of each other, complete 10 days after filing proof; (5) court-ordered alternative method.
- **CPLR 306-b** — Deadline to complete service after filing → 120 days.

## CPLR Article 4 — Special Proceedings

- **CPLR 401-402** — Parties are called Petitioner / Respondent (not plaintiff/defendant).
- **CPLR 402-403** — Petition vs. Notice of Petition — Petition = the actual claim/pleading. Notice of petition = separate document announcing hearing time/place.
- **CPLR 408** — Disclosure (discovery) requires leave of court first — not automatic like a regular action.
- **CPLR 409** — No triable issues of fact at the hearing → summary determination, no trial.
- **CPLR 410** — Triable issues ARE raised → goes to trial "forthwith".

## CPLR Article 5 — Venue

- **CPLR 503(a)** — Default venue → county where a party resides at commencement, OR where a substantial part of events occurred, OR (if no party resides in NY) any county plaintiff designates.
- **CPLR 507** — Real property venue → county where the property is located. Property spanning multiple counties → either county is proper.
- **CPLR 510** — Three grounds for change of venue: (1) improper county designated, (2) impartial trial can't be had there, (3) convenience of material witnesses / ends of justice.
- **CPLR 511** — Timing → demand served with or before the answer → plaintiff has 5 days to consent in writing → if no consent, defendant must move within 15 days of serving the demand.

## CPLR Article 10 — Parties Generally

- **CPLR 1001** — Necessary joinder — party must be joined if their absence would make judgment unfair or incomplete.
- **CPLR 1002** — Permissive joinder — parties may join voluntarily if claims share common questions of law/fact.
- **CPLR 1003** — Nonjoinder (left out someone required) → possible dismissal (without prejudice). Misjoinder (wrongly included someone) → never grounds for dismissal, court just drops that party.
- **CPLR 1006** — Interpleader — a stakeholder holding disputed property forces competing claimants to litigate against each other, then steps out.
- **CPLR 1007** — Third-party practice (impleader) — defendant brings in someone else ("third-party defendant") who may actually be liable, within the same case.
- **CPLR 1012/1013** — Intervention — 1012 = as of right (real, direct interest not otherwise protected). 1013 = permissive (common question of law/fact).
- **CPLR 1015** — Death of a party mid-case → court loses jurisdiction to act until substitution is made.
- **CPLR 1021** — No substitution for a long time → action may be dismissed as to that party for unreasonable delay.
- **CPLR 1004** *[Background]* — Unincorporated associations (e.g. a club) can sue or be sued as an entity, in the name of the association, without naming every individual member.
- **CPLR 1024** *[Background]* — Unknown parties ("John/Jane Doe") — a party who doesn't know someone's real identity can sue them as an unknown party. Requires due diligence to identify the real name first. Interacts with the 306-b 120-day service clock.
- **CPLR 1008–1011** *[Background]* — Nuts-and-bolts procedure for third-party practice (impleader) once initiated under 1007 — answering the third-party complaint, the original defendant's rights, dismissal for delay.
- **CPLR 1014, 1016–1020, 1022–1023, 1025–1026** *[Background]* — Mechanics of substitution beyond death (transfer of interest, incompetency, corporate dissolution, public officer succession) and what happens if a required substitution never happens (dismissal as to that party).

## CPLR Article 11 — Waiver of Costs & Fees

- **CPLR 1101** — Fee waiver at case start — file an affidavit of insufficient means with the initial papers, no separate motion needed. Denied? Case dismissed unless fee paid within 120 days.
- **CPLR 1102** — What the waiver grants — exemption from prepaying costs/fees, free process issuance, possible assigned counsel.
- **CPLR 1103** — If the party later wins money, the court can order the waived fees/costs paid back out of that recovery.

## CPLR Article 21 — Papers

- **CPLR 2102** — Papers are filed with the clerk of the court; the clerk generally can't refuse to accept them just for a defect in form.
- **CPLR 2103(a)** — Who can serve papers in a pending case? Anyone who isn't a party, age 18+.
- **CPLR 2103(b)** — Once a party is represented, papers go to their attorney, not the party directly.
- **CPLR 2103(b)(2)** — Mail service extra time: +5 days if mailed within NY State; +6 days if mailed from outside NY but within the US; overnight delivery adds only 1 business day.
- **CPLR 2104** — Stipulations — binding only if in writing and signed ("subscribed") by the party/attorney, or made orally in open court on the record. Nothing else counts.
- **CPLR 2105** — An attorney can certify a copy as a true copy of the original — no separate notarization needed for that certification.
- **CPLR 2106** — Affirmation vs. affidavit — affidavit requires notarization; an affirmation just needs a signed statement made "under the penalties of perjury," no notary. As of Jan. 1, 2024, any person can use an affirmation (previously limited mainly to attorneys/certain licensed health care practitioners).
- **CPLR 2101** *[Background]* — Baseline formatting rules for court papers (legible, proper caption identifying the court/parties/index number, etc.).
- **CPLR 2101(e)** *[Sample Q]* — Unless a rule specifically requires the original, copies (not originals) may generally be served or filed — this covers affidavits, orders, exhibits, and notes of issue alike. If the original is lost or withheld, the court can authorize a copy to stand in for it. Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026).

## CPLR Article 22 — Stay, Motions, Orders & Mandates

- **CPLR 2214** — Motion timing — notice of motion + supporting papers served at least 8 days before the hearing; answering papers at least 2 days before. (Same 8/2 pattern as CPLR 403 for special proceedings.)
- **CPLR 2221(d)** — Reargue — "court misunderstood the facts/law." No new facts allowed. Must be made within 30 days of service of notice of entry. Denial is not appealable.
- **CPLR 2221(e)** — Renew — based on new facts not offered before (with reasonable excuse) or a change in the law. No strict 30-day deadline. Denial is appealable. Memory hook: reargue = same facts, different argument. Renew = actually new material.
- **CPLR 2215** — Cross-motions — served at least 3 days before the hearing by default, or 7 days before if the original notice of motion (served at least 16 days out) properly demanded it.
- **CPLR 2201** *[Background]* — A court may grant a stay of proceedings "upon such terms as may be just" — broad, general discretion.
- **CPLR 2211** *[Background]* — Defines when a motion is "made" — by serving a notice of motion or an order to show cause.
- **CPLR 2212/2213** *[Background]* — Where a motion gets noticed to be heard — generally the judicial district/county where the action is triable, or an adjoining county.
- **CPLR 2217–2220, 2222–2223** *[Background]* — Procedural mechanics after a motion is decided: disclosing any prior related motion, trying a factual issue raised on a motion, the order's required form and entry/service, docketing a money order as an enforceable judgment, an officer's duty to carry out a court mandate.

## CPLR Article 23 — Subpoenas, Oaths & Affirmations

- **CPLR 2301** — Subpoena = compels testimony. Subpoena duces tecum = compels production of documents/things.
- **CPLR 2302(a)** — An attorney of record can issue a subpoena without a court order — attorneys act as officers of the court for this purpose.
- **CPLR 2304** *[Sample Q]* — Challenging a subpoena = motion to quash, made promptly, in the court where the subpoena is returnable. Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026).
- **CPLR 2308** — Disobedience — judicial subpoena (judge/clerk/attorney-issued): contempt of court; if the witness is a party, pleadings can be struck; penalty up to $150 + damages; sheriff can be sent to bring the witness in. Non-judicial subpoena (agency-issued): must move in Supreme Court to compel; penalty up to $50.
- **CPLR 2303/2303-a** *[Background]* — A subpoena/subpoena duces tecum is served the same way as a summons; a trial subpoena on a party can instead just go to their attorney (per the 2103(b) rules).
- **CPLR 2305** *[Background]* — Sets when/how someone must appear pursuant to a subpoena, including that a custodian can sometimes send records instead of appearing personally.
- **CPLR 2306/2307** *[Background]* — Certified hospital records, or records of a municipal/state department, library, or bureau, can often be produced by certified copy instead of requiring the custodian to testify in person.
- **CPLR 2309** *[Background]* — Who can administer an oath/affirmation — anyone authorized to take acknowledgments of deeds (notaries), or anyone authorized by NY law to receive evidence; a court clerk (or deputy) can specifically swear in jurors.

## CPLR Article 30 — Remedies & Pleading

- **CPLR 3012** — Answer deadline — 20 days after service of the pleading responded to, as the baseline. Extended to 30 days if service was made via CPLR 308(2) or 308(4) — the "substitute service" methods. Direct personal delivery (308(1)) stays at 20 days.
- **CPLR 3025(a)** *[Sample Q]* — Amending a pleading without leave — once, freely, within 20 days of serving it, OR before the response deadline expires, OR within 20 days after the other side responds. After that, need the court's permission or the other side's agreement. Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026), word for word against the statute.
- **CPLR 3018(b)** — Affirmative defenses must be specifically pled in the answer or they're generally waived — e.g. statute of limitations, release, payment, res judicata, fraud, illegality, discharge in bankruptcy, arbitration and award, infancy/disability.
- **CPLR 3011** *[Background]* — Kinds of pleadings — complaint and answer are the basics. Answer may include a counterclaim (vs. plaintiff) or cross-claim (vs. co-defendant). A claim against someone new is a third-party complaint. A counterclaim requires a reply.
- **CPLR 3013–3017** *[Background]* — Pleadings must be detailed enough to give fair notice of events and legal elements ("particularity"); certain claims (fraud, mistake, defamation) require extra specificity; a pleading must include a demand stating relief sought.
- **CPLR 3019** *[Background]* — Counterclaims and cross-claims — counterclaim = defendant's claim back against the plaintiff. Cross-claim = claim against a co-party.
- **CPLR 3020–3024** *[Background]* — Verification = a sworn/affirmed statement a pleading's contents are true. Once one pleading is verified, subsequent pleadings generally must be too. A motion to correct pleadings can address a vague or scandalous pleading.

## CPLR Article 32 — Accelerated Judgment

- **CPLR 3211(a)** — Motion to dismiss — specific numbered grounds, including (1) documentary evidence contradicts the claim, (5) statute of limitations/release/payment/res judicata, (7) fails to state a cause of action, (8) lack of personal jurisdiction.
- **CPLR 3211 waiver trap** — If a defendant makes ANY pre-answer motion to dismiss without including lack-of-personal-jurisdiction, that defense is waived forever. If no motion is made, must be raised in the answer or it's lost. Same logic as CPLR 3018.
- **CPLR 3212(a)** — Summary judgment — moving party must show no genuine issue of material fact and entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. Must be made no later than 120 days after the note of issue is filed, absent good cause (Brill v. City of New York).
- **CPLR 3215(a)/(c)** — Default judgment — plaintiff may seek it when defendant fails to appear/answer, but must take steps to enter it within one year of the default, or the court must dismiss as abandoned, unless sufficient cause is shown.
- **CPLR 3213** *[Background]* — Fast-track summary judgment available only when the claim is based on an instrument for payment of money only, or an existing judgment — skips a full complaint/answer cycle.
- **CPLR 3216** *[Background]* — Want of prosecution — after issue is joined, a party can serve a 90-day demand to file a note of issue; miss it and the court may (not must) dismiss, generally without prejudice.
- **CPLR 3217** *[Background]* — Voluntary discontinuance — plaintiff can generally discontinue by simple notice before the defendant answers, or by stipulation/court order afterward.
- **CPLR 3218–3222** *[Background]* — Narrower mechanisms: judgment by confession, tender, conditional offers to liquidate damages, offers to compromise, deciding a case on submitted facts without a trial.

## CPLR Article 34 — Calendar Practice; Trial Preferences

- **CPLR 3403** *[Sample Q]* — Trial preferences — cases normally tried in note-of-issue filing order, but these jump the line: actions by/against the state or a political subdivision; statutory preference; interests of justice; upon application of a party age 70+ (not automatic); medical/dental/podiatric malpractice; terminally ill plaintiff cases. Confirmed by an official released sample question (Senior Court Clerk, 2026).
- **CPLR 3404** — Dismissal of abandoned cases — marked "off," struck, or unanswered at a calendar call, not restored within one year, deemed abandoned and dismissed for neglect to prosecute. Clerk makes the entry directly — no court order needed. Same one-year pattern as CPLR 3215(c) and 3216.
- **CPLR 3401** *[Background]* — The chief administrator of the courts sets the actual rules for hearing calendars/notes of issue — administrative, not a fixed number to memorize.
- **CPLR 3402** *[Background]* — A case can be placed on the calendar any time after issue is joined, or at least 40 days after service. A note of issue must then be filed with the clerk within 10 days of that; clerk enters the case as of the filing date. A new party gets served the note of issue within 5 days.
- **CPLR 3405** *[Background]* — Small-claim arbitration option for money claims up to $6,000 (most courts) or $10,000 (NYC Civil Court); a party can still demand a full jury trial afterward ("trial de novo").
- **CPLR 3406–3410** *[Background]* — Special mandatory procedures for specific case types — malpractice notice filed within 60 days of issue joined in dental/medical/podiatric cases, plus mandatory pre-calendar/settlement conferences for malpractice, terminally-ill-plaintiff, and residential foreclosure actions.

## CPLR Article 40 — Trial Generally

- **CPLR 4017** — Objections — formal "exceptions" aren't required in NY. A party just needs to state what ruling they wanted, or their objection to what the court did, on the record at the time. Failing to do so can limit review on appeal later.
- **CPLR 4001** *[Background]* — A court can appoint a referee to determine a specific issue, perform an act, or (with the parties' consent) hear, report on, and determine the entire case.
- **CPLR 4011–4015** *[Background]* — Procedural trial mechanics: order in which parties present their case, furnishing marked-up pleadings to the jury, holding trial somewhere other than the courthouse (e.g. a jury view), and the court's ability to set a time limit on trial length.
- **CPLR 4018** *[Background]* — Where a statute allows increased (multiplied) damages, the verdict must specify the single-damages amount separately, and judgment is then entered for the increased sum.
- **CPLR 4019** *[Background]* — Restricts recording in-camera interviews of children in custody-related proceedings.

## CPLR Article 41 — Trial by Jury

- **CPLR 4104** — Number of jurors — a civil jury is composed of 6 persons, not 12. Different from criminal juries (covered later in CPL) — keep these separate from the start.
- **CPLR 4102** — Demand and waiver — jury trial isn't automatic. Demand by filing a note of issue containing a jury demand. If filed without one, another party has 15 days from service of that note of issue to demand a jury themselves. No demand from anyone = waived for everyone (bench trial instead). A jury demand can't be withdrawn without the other side's consent.
- **CPLR 4113** — Disagreement by jury — civil verdicts do not need to be unanimous. A verdict can be rendered by 5 of 6 jurors (five-sixths). Only if even that many can't agree, after being kept together as long as the court finds reasonable, is a mistrial declared and a new trial ordered before a different jury. NY's constitution was amended in 1935 specifically to allow this.
- **CPLR 4101** *[Background]* — Issues triable by a jury as of right include: money-only judgment actions, ejectment, dower, waste, nuisance, chattel recovery, RPAPL Article 15 real property claims, or wherever the constitution/a statute grants a jury right. Equitable claims/defenses go to the court, not a jury.
- **CPLR 4103** *[Background]* — The same demand/waiver mechanism from CPLR 4102 applies even when a jury-triable issue is only revealed once trial has already begun.
- **CPLR 4105–4106** *[Background]* — The first 6 jurors approved and sworn constitute the jury. Alternate jurors may be added on a party's request with the court's consent — generally up to 2, unless the court orders otherwise.
- **CPLR 4107–4108** *[Background]* — A judge must be present during jury examination (voir dire). Challenges can be made "to the array" (the whole panel) or to individual jurors.
- **CPLR 4109** *[Background]* — Peremptory challenges — each side gets 3 combined (regardless of how many parties are on that side) plus 1 for every 2 alternate jurors. If one side has more parties than the other, the court may grant an equal number of additional challenges to both sides.
- **CPLR 4110, 4110-a/b/c** *[Background]* — Challenges for cause — e.g. a juror employed by a party, or a disqualifying relationship. Related niche rules: village-resident juror competency, objecting to jury instructions at the time given, and jury "viewing" of premises (the defendant may, but need not, be present).
- **CPLR 4111–4112** *[Background]* — A jury can return a general verdict, a special verdict, or answer written interrogatories. CPLR 4112 governs the formal entry of the verdict once rendered.

## CPLR Article 42 — Trial by the Court

- **CPLR 4211** — Any issue not required to be tried by a jury is decided by the court itself (a judge), unless referred to a referee to determine.
- **CPLR 4212** — The court can optionally submit an issue it must decide to an advisory jury (non-binding, just guidance) or, in matters of account or exceptional circumstances, to a referee to report back. The judge still makes the final call either way.
- **CPLR 4213** — The court's decision (oral or written) must state the essential facts it relied on. In personal injury, property damage, or wrongful death cases specifically, the damages award must be itemized by category (medical expenses, lost earnings, pain and suffering, etc.) — not a single lump figure.
- **CPLR 4201** *[Background]* — A referee assigned to inquire and report has the power to issue subpoenas, administer oaths, and direct the parties into disclosure proceedings to speed up resolution of the issues.

## CPLR Article 43 — Trial by a Referee

- **CPLR 4317** — When a referee can determine (not just report on) an issue — (a) by consent: parties stipulate, clerk enters the order directly once filed, except matrimonial actions, certain corporate dissolution/receivership actions, or cases with an infant defendant, which still need the court's designation. (b) Without consent: court can order it, most often for examination of a long account (e.g. mechanic's lien foreclosure) or a severable damages issue not requiring a jury.
- **CPLR 4301, 4311–4316** *[Background]* — A referee's authority once appointed, the formal order of reference, number/qualifications of referees (generally one), notice to the parties, appointing a successor if needed, the referee being sworn before proceeding, and procedure if more than one referee is used.
- **CPLR 4318–4321** *[Background]* — The referee conducts the trial much like a court trial; a reference "to determine" produces a binding decision, while a reference "to report" is advisory and subject to court confirmation; referee fees/expenses are fixed by the court and generally paid by the parties.

## CPLR Article 44 — Trial Motions

- **CPLR 4401** — Motion for judgment during trial (a "directed verdict") — made after the opposing party's evidence on that claim/issue closes, or any time based on admissions. Standard: no rational process by which the jury could find for the non-moving party.
- **CPLR 4404/4406** — Post-trial motion for judgment and new trial — same "no rational process" standard, applied after a verdict comes in. 4404(a) = jury cases, 4404(b) = non-jury cases. CPLR 4406 limits a party to a single post-trial motion — no serial attempts.
- **CPLR 4402** *[Sample Q]* — At any time during the trial, the court, on motion of any party, may order a continuance or a new trial in the interest of justice on such terms as may be just. Confirmed word-for-word by an official released sample question (2026) — previously under-prioritized here as "supplementary"; corrected given real evidence it's tested.
- **CPLR 4401-a, 4403, 4405** *[Background]* — A specific rule for medical malpractice claims based solely on lack of informed consent — judgment for the defendant must be granted at the end of plaintiff's case if the plaintiff failed to produce expert medical testimony supporting the claim. After a referee's report or advisory jury verdict, a party can move to confirm, reject, or seek other relief (4403). CPLR 4405 sets the timing and which judge hears a post-trial motion.

## CPLR Article 12 — Infants, Incompetents & Conservatees

- **CPLR 1201** *[Sample Q]* — How an infant appears in an action: by the guardian of their property; if none, by a parent with legal custody; if none, by another person/agency with legal custody; if the infant is married, by an adult spouse residing with them. (Similar structure applies to incompetent persons via a committee, and conservatees via a conservator.) Confirmed by an official released sample question (2026) even though Article 12 isn't named in the official subject matter list — proof the "and any other statutes... referred to therein" language is real, not just boilerplate.

## CPLR Article 50 — Judgments Generally

- **CPLR 5001** — "Interest to verdict" — covers the period before a verdict, from the earliest ascertainable date the claim existed. Only applies to contract breach and property-deprivation actions — NOT personal injury or most other torts. Equitable actions leave rate/date to the court's discretion.
- **CPLR 5002** — "Interest from verdict to judgment" — once there's a verdict/report/decision, interest runs on the full awarded sum until judgment is entered. Applies more broadly than 5001 — not limited to contract/property cases.
- **CPLR 5003** *[Sample Q]* — "Interest upon judgment" (post-judgment) — every money judgment bears interest from its date of entry; an order directing payment of money that's been docketed as a judgment bears interest from the date of docketing. Confirmed by TWO official released sample questions (2026) — Senior Court Clerk Q17 and Associate Court Clerk Q1.
- **CPLR 5004(a)** *[Sample Q]* — The interest rate for all three stages above (5001/5002/5003) is 9% per annum, unless a different statute specifically sets another rate. Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).
- **CPLR 5015(a)** — Relief from judgment (vacatur) — five grounds: (1) excusable default — has its own one-year deadline from service of the judgment + notice of entry; (2) newly discovered evidence that would probably change the outcome; (3) fraud/misrepresentation/misconduct by the other side; (4) lack of jurisdiction — NOT bound by the one-year clock that applies to ground 1; (5) reversal/modification/vacatur of a prior judgment this one was based on.

## CPLR Article 52 — Enforcement of Money Judgments

- **CPLR 5222** — Restraining notice — freezes (doesn't yet seize) a debtor's property/funds. Issued without a court order, by the court clerk, the creditor's attorney (as officer of the court), or a support collection unit. Exempt from restraint: Social Security, SSI, public assistance, unemployment, workers' comp, pensions, and 90% of wages earned in the last 60 days. Generally holds about a year against a garnishee; a second restraint on the same account needs the court's permission.
- **CPLR 5230(c)** *[Sample Q]* — Execution — directs a sheriff/marshal to actually seize property to satisfy a judgment. Must be returned to the clerk of the issuing court within 60 days (extendable once by the creditor's attorney). Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).

## CPLR Article 55 — Appeals Generally

- **CPLR 5512** — Only a judgment or an order can be appealed — not a bare decision or verdict standing alone.
- **CPLR 5513** — Deadline to appeal: 30 days after being served with a copy of the judgment/order together with notice of entry — same trigger phrase as CPLR 2221's reargument deadline, not just bare service of the judgment.
- **CPLR 5511** — Only an "aggrieved party" can appeal — someone the judgment/order actually harmed. A full winner generally can't appeal just to challenge the reasoning. One more limit: an aggrieved party generally can't appeal a judgment entered on their own default — that's what a CPLR 5015 vacatur motion is for instead.
- **CPLR 5514** *[Background]* — If a party's attorney dies, is removed, suspended, or becomes incapacitated during the appeal window, the time to appeal is extended to 60 days from that event — echoes the CPLR 210(b) death-of-a-party toll from Article 2, just applied to the attorney instead.
- **CPLR 5519** *[Background]* — Taking an appeal can trigger an automatic stay of enforcement in certain circumstances — e.g. the state or a political subdivision as appellant, or posting an undertaking (bond) to secure the judgment pending appeal.

## CPLR Article 70 — Habeas Corpus

- **CPLR 7002** — Who can petition, and where — the detained person themselves, or someone acting on their behalf, may petition. It can go to the supreme court in the judicial district where the person is detained, OR to any justice of the supreme court — not locked to one specific courthouse.
- **CPLR 7003** — The court must issue the writ without delay, on any day — no normal business-day limitation. 7003(c): a judge (or each assenting member of a court) who wrongfully refuses to issue a valid writ personally forfeits $1,000 to the detained person. 7003(b): a court need not re-issue a writ if the same detention's legality was already litigated with no new grounds, and the ends of justice wouldn't be served by granting it again.
- **CPLR 7001, 7004–7008** *[Background]* — Article 70 covers common law/statutory habeas corpus writs and common law certiorari to inquire into detention (7001). The writ specifies who it's issued for/directed to and when returnable (7004); may be served on any day (7005); the person served must obey it (7006); a warrant can precede/accompany the writ if there's proof the person may be removed from the court's reach (7007); the detaining party files a "return" explaining the detention (7008).
- **CPLR 7009** *[Background]* — Hearing — where detention is by virtue of a mandate, written notice of the hearing must be served 8 days before it (personally), unless the court orders otherwise. The hearing itself must proceed in a summary manner — no unnecessary delay.
- **CPLR 7010–7012** *[Background]* — If illegally detained, the judgment directs discharge "forthwith" (7010). An appeal can be taken from a judgment refusing to grant the writ (7011). CPLR 7012 addresses redetention after a discharge.

## CPLR Article 78 — Proceeding Against Body/Officer

- **CPLR 7803** — The four questions an Article 78 petition can raise: (1) mandamus to compel — did the agency fail a non-discretionary legal duty? (2) prohibition — is the agency acting without/in excess of jurisdiction? (3) mandamus to review — was the determination made in violation of lawful procedure, affected by legal error, or arbitrary and capricious/an abuse of discretion? (4) certiorari — after an evidentiary hearing, is the determination supported by substantial evidence on the whole record?
- **CPLR 217** — Statute of limitations for Article 78 — 4 months (not the 1/3/6-year buckets from Article 2), running from when the determination becomes final and binding on the petitioner. A much tighter window than ordinary civil claims.
- **CPLR 7804(b)** *[Sample Q]* — An Article 78 proceeding must be brought in Supreme Court. Confirmed by an official released sample question (Associate Court Clerk, 2026).
- **CPLR 7804(g)** — When the 4th theory (substantial evidence) is raised, Supreme Court doesn't decide that piece itself — it first resolves anything that could end the case outright (jurisdiction, statute of limitations, res judicata). If the case survives, the substantial-evidence question is transferred to the Appellate Division, which disposes of it directly as the first-level decision-maker on that question.
- **CPLR 7801–7802, 7805–7806** *[Background]* — Article 78 replaced the old common-law writs of certiorari/mandamus/prohibition with one unified proceeding (7801); parties are called petitioner/respondent, same special-proceeding terminology as Article 4 (7802); the court can stay further agency action pending the outcome (7805); judgment may include restitution/damages incidental to the primary relief, and costs (7806).
